After Hours: three looks for the museum

The museum still wears grey brick from cellar to roof. Below are three looks for the whole of it, each built into the level and photographed from the same 58 cameras as the level is now. A look changes textures only: no wall, floor or railing moves, and the level plays the same.

Beyond paint: options to talk about

None of these is in the pictures. Each changes the level's shape, lighting or how it plays, so I would rather agree them first.

  1. After hours. The museum is evenly bright. It is shut for the night: dim rooms with a pool of light on each case and picture would suit all three looks, and the night roof over the hall. It makes the snipers on the mezzanine, the upper gallery and the roof harder to see.
  2. Statues. The four plinths in the great hall carry glass cases. A statue on each would be a new thing drawn for the level, and it blocks shots.
  3. The roof's edge. The roof stands against a taller wall on three sides. A parapet with sky and a horizon beyond it would make it a roof; that edge would be rebuilt.
  4. The frames. A frame is a dark doorway with a dark column over it. In rooms hung with pictures it could have a lamp or an empty gilt frame over it; that helps more with four Stray doors about.
  5. Fewer exhibits. Every free wall has two or three. Fewer would make each room calmer and each exhibit a landmark.
  6. Room tints are gone in the looks, as in MAP01 to MAP05: the coloured column over each far side, the torches and the doors carry the colour. Here the two wall colours in each wing do some of that work.
  7. The earlier levels' secrets. If you take the dressed cellar, grotto, shed and stockroom, the same could be done for the stores and secrets of MAP03 to MAP05.
  8. Mixing. Outside, hall, wings and rooms are textured apart, so the court of one look with the wings of another costs nothing.
