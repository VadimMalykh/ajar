Lost Property: three looks for the station, six for the nailer

The terminus still wears grey brick from top to bottom, and the nailer, who first appears here, still wears its prototype sprite. Below are three looks for the whole station, each built into the level and photographed from the same 50 cameras as the level is now, and after them six candidates for the nailer, posed in the goods yard and in its shed.

The three looks

Plan of the station, with camera numbers

One grid square is 128 map units; north is up, and you start in the vestibule at the bottom left. Places above platform level are drawn darker, the tracks grey, each with its floor height. The store is reached only by door, so it is not on the plan.

The nailer

It walks to a hung door and nails it to its frame; nobody can take that door down until the nailer is dead. With no door left to nail it comes for you with the hammer. The lodger became the shadow of a revenant and the thief the shadow of an arch-vile, so four of the five new candidates are shadows too, each of a broader monster, with two bright nail heads where the others have brass plates.

Each is shown in the level as it is now: three poses in the goods yard, one at the frame in the engine shed where you first meet it, and dead. The shed is dark, which is the hard case for a shadow.

Beyond paint: options to talk about

None of these is in the pictures. Each changes the level's shape, lighting or how it plays, so I would rather agree them first.

  1. Light. The station is evenly bright under Doom's dusk sky. Darker under the canopies and in the concourse, with the lit windows and lamps doing the work, would suit all three looks. It makes the snipers on the bridge and the gallery harder to see.
  2. Sky beside the bridge. The bridge has a three-storey wall along its east side. A low parapet with the sky and a horizon beyond it would make it a bridge and not a corridor. It needs that edge rebuilt.
  3. A platform edge. A white line along both platforms, a strip of its own floor. It would also mark the drop to the tracks.
  4. The train. One solid block with a painted side. Doors that line up with the platform, a locomotive at the east end, or a second, shorter train at platform 1 are all blocks or textures; a second train changes the fight on the tracks.
  5. Station things. A clock over the concourse's mouth, a departure board, luggage trolleys, a name board on the canopies. The clock and boards are textures; trolleys are blocks that change where monsters walk.
  6. The frames. Under the canopies a frame is a dark doorway among several doors. A lamp or a sign over each would help, more so with Stray doors about.
  7. Room tints are gone in the looks, as in MAP01 to MAP04: the coloured column over each far side, the torches and the doors carry the colour. Say if you miss them here, where six doors and four Stray doors are in play.
  8. The nailer beyond its sprite. A hammering sound that carries, so you hear a door being nailed across the station; whether nails show on the HUD; whether a third shadow is one too many. Its voice is still the revenant's, as the lodger's and the thief's.
  9. Mixing. Outside, concourse and rooms are textured apart, so the fronts of one look with the rooms of another cost nothing.
