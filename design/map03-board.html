Beyond paint: options to talk about

None of these is in the pictures. Each changes the level's shape or lighting, so I would rather agree them first.

  1. A proscenium arch. The stage is the east end of one big room, so nothing frames it. An arch (a wall across with a tall opening, and the curtain drawn up behind it) would make it a stage at first sight. It narrows the view between stage and gods, which changes the fights across the house.
  2. Seats with backs. Rows are low blocks you can walk over. Backs would be a second, higher strip behind each row: they read as seats, give cover, and stop you crossing rows except by the aisles.
  3. A chandelier and house lights. Light is even everywhere. The auditorium could be dim with a bright stage, lamps along the tier fronts and one big chandelier (a sprite or a lowered piece of ceiling). Dim suits the lodger, which wakes here.
  4. Boxes. Two boxes beside the stage would be the most theatre-like thing missing. A box is also a natural place for a far side or a sniper.
  5. Room tints are gone in the looks. Circle, gods, flies and the rooms were tinted after the door that leads there. As in MAP01 and MAP02, the looks leave that to the coloured column over each far side, the torches and the doors. With six doors here, say if you miss the tints as a guide.
  6. Front and back. All three looks share one back of house. The other way is possible: a plain front and a rich back, or the back following the front.
  7. Doom's props. Candelabra suit Opera and Playhouse, tech lamps suit Civic; none suits all three. They are unchanged for now.
  8. Mixing. Each part is textured on its own, so the foyer of one look with the auditorium of another costs nothing. Seat, curtain and carpet colours can also be swapped within a look.
