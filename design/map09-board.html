Putting You Through: three looks for the telephone exchange, and six for the repainter

The exchange still wears grey brick from forecourt to roof. Below are three looks for the whole of it, each built into the level and photographed from the same 48 cameras as the level is now. A look changes textures only: no wall, floor or railing moves, and the level plays the same.

The repainter, new in this level, still has a provisional look. Its six candidates follow the rooms.

Picks can be mixed, for example the switchroom from one look and the wings from another. Your picks are collected near the bottom as one line to paste into the chat. You can also comment on this page. Click a picture to enlarge it.

The three looks

Plan of the exchange, with camera numbers

One grid square is 128 map units; north is up, and you start in the forecourt at the bottom. Upper floors are drawn darker and what lies below ground grey, each with its floor height; secrets are pink. The gallery and the balcony stand over the switchroom, the cable loft over nothing, and the roof over the west wing. Cameras 40 to 42 are on the line, which no look touches; 11 and 18 are blocked by furniture and are replaced by 43 and 44.

The repainter

It goes about the switchroom and does not fight. It walks to a door, hung or leaning, and takes its colour; then to a second door, and the two change places for good. The colour it carries is the warning: kill it with a colour on its brush and nothing has changed. The lodger, the thief and the nailer are each the shadow of one of Doom's monsters with a pair of eyes; the repainter was built as an imp's shadow without eyes and has not been chosen.

Each candidate is one of Doom's own sprites. Poses two to four show it with BLUE on its brush, which is what a look has to make plain.

Beyond paint: options to talk about

None of these is in the pictures. Each changes the level's shape, lighting or how it plays, so I would rather agree them first.

  1. Lights out. Indoors is evenly bright. On the night shift the hall could be dim, with a lamp over each rank of boards and the boards' own lamps glowing, as MAP07 and MAP08 are now; the call office and the manager's office would stay lit. It makes the chaingunners on the gallery harder to see.
  2. Signs. CANTEEN, APPARATUS, TRUNKS, MANAGER and the rest on a plate over each door, as in MAP08. It would help to keep the two wings apart when carrying a door through them.
  3. Things on the floor. Operators' chairs with a headset left on each, the supervisor's desk, a wall clock, a trolley of cable: sprites that stop nothing, as the dummies and the till are in MAP08.
  4. The forecourt. It is a walled yard. It could have railings and a gate on the road with the street going by, as MAP08's arcade has now; the kiosk could be a lit call box.
  5. The roof's edge. Walled in on all sides. A parapet with the town beyond, and wires running off a mast to the poles, would open it as MAP08's roof was opened; its edges would be rebuilt.
  6. Sound. A telephone ringing somewhere that nobody answers; the clatter of selectors in the apparatus room; a bell when the chain is made.
  7. The switchboards under the gallery's gap. Still open from when the level was built: a jump down from the gap lands on a board.
  8. Room tints are gone in the looks, as in MAP01 to MAP08: the coloured column over each far side, the torches and the doors carry the colour.
  9. Mixing. The front, the call office, the switchroom, the trunk room and each room of the wings are textured apart, so the switchroom of one look with the wings of another costs nothing. Switchboards, racks and call boxes can be chosen apart from the walls too.

Beyond the sprite: the repainter

  1. The warning. Now a line on screen says which door it has touched, and the shadow turns. Looks 2 and 6 lean on the line; say if a sound should go with it, or drips of the colour on the floor behind it.
  2. Seen at work. The thief must be seen before it may take a door down. The repainter may swap two doors with nobody looking. Say if it should be held to the thief's rule.
  3. Voice. It borrows the imp's sounds. A different set from Doom's costs nothing, and none at all is a choice too.
  4. Size. The thief is six tenths of an arch-vile. Any of these can be made smaller or larger.
  5. Shown here, not built. These are posed photographs before the switchroom's frame, in the level as it is now, with GREEN hung and BLUE leaning beside it. The repainter in the game is unchanged until you choose.
